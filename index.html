Critical findings

🚫
The whole process happened without neighbors knowing
The county purchased the land in 2021¹, selected an operator in 2024, approved a 20-year lease², and issued construction permits — all without a single public hearing. Community Development Director Ann Lippmann confirmed this in writing on April 20, 2026.³ Because the property has been zoned industrial since 1969, no rezoning was needed — and without rezoning, no hearing was ever legally required.
⏰
There are no county rules on hours, trucks, or odor
The county applied zero operational conditions during plan review.³ What time trucks can arrive, where they stage, how odor is managed — none of it is governed by anything Paulding County can enforce. Rules fall entirely to the private Capital Waste Services contract⁴ and GA EPD state rules⁵ — neither of which neighbors had seen until records were requested.
🚛
No traffic study was done, despite a weight-limit road
The NW Georgia Regional Commission exempted this project from a Development of Regional Impact review — so no traffic study was ever required.⁶ Rosedale Drive, the primary truck access road, is a two-lane residential road with a strict 18-ton weight limit.⁷ Despite no formal study, GDOT submitted at least five separate rounds of comments on the project⁸ — suggesting traffic is far more contested than the county has acknowledged publicly. No finalized GDOT plan has been made public.
☣️
The county's own attorney flagged a cancer-chemical risk
At the April 14, 2026 Work Session⁹, the county attorney raised PFAS ("forever chemicals") contamination as a real risk. The county retained the law firm Arnold and Nitkin LLP on contingency to pursue litigation against PFAS manufacturers — an implicit admission that contamination of groundwater is a foreseeable outcome at county waste facilities.
🚧
Construction violated a stream buffer and triggered a stop work order
On April 6, 2026, a county inspector issued both a Stop Work Order and a Stream Buffer Violation — meaning work had been done inside the protected buffer zones near the creek.¹⁰ The site was marked "in compliance" by April 14¹¹, but no corrective action documentation has been made public. Weekly inspections continue as of May 12, 2026.¹²
📄
Key documents in the plan set contradict each other
The area of land to be disturbed is listed as 12.24 acres in hydrology reports¹³ and 10.86 acres in clearing and grading submittals¹⁴ — a 1.38-acre gap that determines erosion control scope and stormwater bonding. Which figure was used for the Land Disturbance Permit¹⁵ is not publicly confirmed. Phase 3 of the Erosion Control Plan was flagged as "TBD" in two rounds of county review comments¹⁶ before the LDP was issued.
📣
Neighbors across two counties are organized and on record
Over 1,660 people signed the Change.org petition.¹⁷ Menllo Station (Paulding) and Warren Creek (Cobb County) residents both spoke publicly.¹⁸ Powder Springs Councilmember Raja Antone formally demanded relocation. Cobb County Commissioner Monique Sheffield is actively working with Paulding officials.¹⁹ The Ozora Road transfer station in Gwinnett County was completely withdrawn after similar community opposition — that's a real precedent.²⁰

Five concrete actions, in order of priority. The Capital Waste contract now has a documented Hours of Operation addendum — whether it's enforceable and whether it's enough are the immediate questions. Start there.
1
Read the Hours of Operation addendum — then find out what it actually covers
Addendum No. 1 to the Capital Waste Services contract specifically governs hours of operation.¹ This is the single most important document for day-to-day quality of life — it's what stands between neighbors and 3 AM truck staging. Get it via Open Records Request if you haven't already (it may be in the public folder). Once you have it, three questions matter:
· What hours does it allow? If trucks are permitted before 6–7 AM, that's the fight.
· Who enforces it? County? Capital Waste self-reporting? GA EPD? Enforcement gaps are as important as the hours themselves.
· What's the penalty for violation? A contract term with no consequence is not a protection.

Also request at the same time (O.C.G.A. § 50-18-70 — county must respond within 3 business days):
· GA EPD Permit by Rule, Section 391-3-4-.06(3)(b)²
· Phase 3 Final Erosion Control Plan and its submission date vs. the Dec 22 LDP³
· The official LOD figure used for the LDP — 12.24 or 10.86 acres?⁴
· All GDOT comment rounds and any final GDOT sign-off⁵
File with: Paulding County Clerk's Office Response required: 3 business days
↓ Contract terms in hand — then push the traffic problem into the open
2
Force the GDOT traffic issue into a public answer
GDOT submitted at least five rounds of comments on this project⁵ — an unusually high number that signals unresolved concerns. Yet no traffic study was ever required, because the Regional Commission exempted the project from DRI review.⁶ These two facts together create real pressure: GDOT's own review flagged issues repeatedly, but the process that would have forced a public traffic study was bypassed entirely.
At the next BOC meeting, ask on the record:
· What did GDOT's comment rounds identify, and what changes were required?
· Is there a finalized GDOT driveway permit or road-use agreement for Rosedale Drive?
· Who pays for road damage if trucks exceed the 18-ton weight limit?⁷

Contact GDOT District 7 (covers Paulding County) directly to ask whether a driveway permit was required and obtained for the transfer station entrance.
GDOT comment rounds 1, 2, 3 & 5 are in public records — request them all

↓ Violations + PFAS admission = coalition power for winnable demands
5
Use the county's own PFAS admission and organize around specific demands
The county attorney's April 14, 2026 statement is on video¹²: by retaining Arnold & Nitkin LLP on contingency against PFAS manufacturers, the county acknowledged contamination at waste facilities is a foreseeable risk. Those are their words. The cross-county coalition — 1,660+ signatures¹³, Menllo Station, Warren Creek, Councilmember Antone, Commissioner Sheffield — represents real political weight. The Ozora Road station in Gwinnett County was withdrawn entirely after sustained community pressure.¹⁴ Focus on demands that can be put in writing and enforced:
· Publicly release the full Hours of Operation addendum and all contract enforcement terms
· Expanded wooded buffer zones beyond the 75-ft minimum
· Mandatory PFAS baseline water testing on nearby wells and waterways before operations begin
· A formal, public GDOT-approved traffic plan for Rosedale Drive
· County-level operating conditions — noise limits, odor monitoring — codified in a public agreement, not just a private contract
· Annual public meeting with Capital Waste Services and county staff
